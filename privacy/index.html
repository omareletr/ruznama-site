RūznāmaPrivacy policy · 8 November 2026

Rūznāma never sees what you write.

There's no account, no ads and no server of mine. What you write stays on your devices and in your own iCloud. The app counts how often it's used, and you can turn that off.

Rūznāma is made by Omar El-Etr, one person. This page says what the app does with what you give it.

Your tasks

Tasks, sections, habits, History and settings are kept on your iPhone. If iCloud is on for Rūznāma, they sync through your private iCloud database and iCloud key-value storage, which only your Apple Account can read. I can't see them. iCloud is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Location

If you allow it, Rūznāma uses your approximate location to work out prayer times on your iPhone. It's rounded to about 1 km and kept on the device. Apple's maps service is asked for the name of your town, and searched when you look a place up by name. A place you choose by name syncs through your iCloud so your devices agree. Without location the app works the same, without prayer times.

Your voice

When you tap the mic, what you say is turned into words on your iPhone, by Apple's on-device speech recognition. The recording is never saved or sent anywhere. The written task syncs like any other.

Sorting tasks

Quick capture sorts a task into your sections with Apple's on-device model where your iPhone has Apple Intelligence, and otherwise by matching words. Either way it happens on the device.

Widgets, Share and Siri

The app leaves a short summary of the day for its widgets and share card in a folder they share on your iPhone. When you share a web page to Rūznāma, the share card fetches that page's title from its website, as Safari would. Requests through Siri and Shortcuts are handled by Apple.

How often it's used

So I can see how many people use Rūznāma and whether they come back, the app on iPhone and Mac sends counts to PostHog, an analytics service: that the app was opened, a task was checked off, a habit was kept, or a task was added with quick capture or Siri. Each count carries your device's model, screen size, system, language, time zone and kind of connection (Wi-Fi or mobile), and the app's version. PostHog also works out a town and country from your internet address. Your device's own location is never used.

You're a random code made when the app is installed, with no name, email or account behind it, so I can count people without knowing who they are. Never sent: what you write, the names of your sections or habits, your device's location or your prayer times.

To stop it, turn off Settings ▸ Rūznāma ▸ Share usage.

What Rūznāma doesn't do

  • No tracking, and no data shared with advertisers or data brokers.
  • No crash-reporting code from anyone else, and no analytics beyond the counts above.
  • Nothing sold, ever.

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may pass me anonymous crash reports. That's Apple's setting, and you can turn it off.

Children

Rūznāma collects nothing that could tell anyone who you are, children included.

Removing your data

Settings ▸ Rūznāma ▸ Export the Book saves everything as one Markdown file. Deleting the app removes what's on the device. To remove the iCloud copy, open iPhone Settings ▸ your name ▸ iCloud ▸ Manage Account Storage, choose Rūznāma and delete its data.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page will say so and its date will change.
